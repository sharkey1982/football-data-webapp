The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2023.

REVIEW OF BUSINESS
The 2022/23 season was the ninety-first in Wigan Athletic Football Club's proud history. However, following a season of chaos and upheaval on and off the pitch, it was almost its last.

After the highs of the 2021/22 season, which resulted in promotion to the Championship, the 2022/23 season was beset with problems before a ball was even kicked. A season brimming with optimism became hugely challenging, testing the resilience of the Club and the Wigan community.

In June 2022 the first signs of financial difficulties became publicly evident. WAFC staff and players were due to be paid before the Jubilee Bank Holiday, and instead had to wait until after the long weekend to receive their wages.

The following month in early July, the salary deadline was missed for a second time with Chief Executive Malachy Brannigan telling journalists:

"Due to an issue beyond our control, I informed staff on Friday that their pay would not hit their accounts until Monday, despite a full day of trying to resolve the issue. I am really mindful of this club's recent past, so we felt it was important we addressed the issue publicly to avoid unnecessary speculation and alarm."

"The payments processes have been changed, so this will not occur again. The Club has offered to assist any staff who have suffered immediate hardship as a result of the delay. The financial health of the Club is strong and stable."

After being crowned League One Champions, Latics bid farewell to Gavin Massey at the end of his playing contract, and Tom Bayliss and Kell Watts returned to their parent clubs. James Mclean signed a one-year contract extension to keep him at the Club for the 2022/23 season. Anthony Scully was signed on a permanent contract from Lincoln City and was joined by Ryan Nyambe on a free transfer. Nathan Broadhead, Ashley Fletcher and Rarmani Edmonds-Green were recruited on loan for the Championship campaign.

Early season results were promising, with the team continuing its momentum from the previous season. By mid-October 2022 the team had won five matches, drawn four and lost four, standing in ninth position in the Championship table.

However, owner funding arrived late for a third time for October 2022 salaries, causing further alarm. This coincided with a downturn in form of the First Team: a run of six defeats in the next seven matches. Leam Richardson was subsequently dismissed as Manager just weeks after signing a new long-term deal.

On 29 November 2022, Kolo Touré was appointed WAFC Manager. Just 59 days later, Touré, along with his backroom staff Kevin Betsy and Ashvir Singh Johal, were dismissed after failing to win any of their nine games, leaving the team bottom of the Championship table and four points from safety.

The Club came to an Agreed Decision with the EFL in January 2023 relating to the late payment of wages on three occasions. A suspended three point deduction was imposed, along with a requirement for the Club's owner, Mr. Abdulrahman Al Jasmi, to deposit an amount equal to 125% of the monthly wage bill into a designated bank account. These funds would be used to pay salaries in the event of any further funding delays.

The January 2023 window saw the playing squad strengthened, with the additions of Steven Caulker on a free transfer together with Omar Rekik, Miguel Azeez, Christ Tiehi, Martin Kelly and Danel Sinani on loan.

On 28th January 2023, Shaun Maloney was appointed as the new Wigan Athletic Manager. Shaun brought with him the experienced Graham Barrow and Max Rogers. Shaun's appointment provided for an immediate improvement in results, with one defeat in the next six games.

Just as it seemed that the Club had turned a corner, and that things had stabilised, there was a real hammer blow in March 2023, when, once again, funding failed to arrive from Mr. Abdulrahman Al Jasmi in time to pay salaries. In accordance with the EFL's Agreed Decision, a three point deduction was immediately imposed, making the prospect of Championship survival remote.

On the 24th March 2023, the Club parted company with Chief Executive Malachy Brannigan after his two years in charge. The Club's Chairman, Mr Talal Al Hammad penned an open letter to the Club's supporters telling them that "The Club have chosen to move in a new direction and a decision on Mal's successor will be announced in due course". He also addressed the issue of funding and late payment of wages:

"I think it is important to be as honest and open with you as possible."

"As the Club statement dated 10 March stated, we had a cash flow issue which caused the delay."

"This issue has now been resolved and I can assure you that the late payment of wages will not happen again. It simply cannot happen again."

"I appreciate you have heard that message from me before and as I said from day one and as you have rightly reminded me, "actions speak louder than words."

"That is something I now must prove to you and I must rebuild trust with our players, staff and of course, you, the fans."

The Chairman Mr. Al Hammad also went on to explain that the Club would be financially responsible going forward and that "By investing in our Academy, we believe we will not only be securing the future of our Club, but also continuing to create a clear pathway for our talented young players to make it through to the first team."

"We are confident that this approach will be a key factor in ensuring the long-term success of our Club." This had been the overarching objective of the plans submitted by Mr. Abdulrahman Al Jasmi's ownership group, to the EFL as part of its bid to take control of the Club from the administrators. The plans quickly changed as a policy ensued of recruiting experienced players in an attempt to gain promotion from League One to the Championship creating huge financial stress once promotion was achieved.

Following the departure of Malachy Brannigan, Tom Markham was appointed 'acting' Chief Executive. Chairman Al Hammad visited the UK the following week and held a meeting with supporter groups to provide them with assurances that Al Jasmi's ownership group would continue to fund the Club's losses.

Relegation to League One was finally confirmed on 29th April 2023 after a 1-1 draw away against Reading.

Despite previous promises, ownership funding failed to arrive again to pay wages in early May 2023. The players took to the field in the final game of the 2022/23 season against Rotherham, with many of them not having being paid the previous week.

The future of Wigan Athletic Football Club was now hanging in the balance. In just over two years, the Club had been rescued from administration, with a very low cost base, presenting a real opportunity to make strides to being self-sustainable, to yet once again fighting for its existence.

On the 19 May 2023, the EFL imposed an additional three point deduction on the Championship points tally, resulting in a final league tally of thirty nine points, ten points from safety. Of more concern for the Club, its supporters and the community was the further four point sanction the EFL imposed to take effect from the start of the following 2023/24 season in League One. The EFL also ruled that in the event Mr Al Jasmi's ownership group failed to deposit the wages bond under the March 2023 Agreed Decision the sanction would increase to an eight point deduction.

Chairman Al Hammad again sought to assure fans that despite this setback, the future was now secure. A statement published on the Club's website explained that "…an eight-figure sum is currently being processed and is due to land in the Club account imminently. This funding will also ensure financial stability for June and the 2023/24 season."

By the 26 May 2023, the wage bond had still not been deposited into the reserve account, and the EFL triggered the further four point deduction. The Latics would now start the 2023/24 season in League One on minus eight points.

There was clearly no sign of any further funding arriving from Mr Al Jasmi's ownership group. Initial advice from insolvency practitioners indicated that given the severity of the amounts owed to creditors, particularly HMRC for PAYE, and the large future commitments on player contracts, administration would probably not be feasible. There was now a real possibility that the EFL may be unable to include the Club in the League One fixture release, only a few weeks away.

The Club's aging squad and its low underlying transfer value, would not provide enough liquidity to fund the losses in the short term until a buyer could be found. The sale of young players, which had ultimately saved the Club during 2020/21 administration, would not come to the rescue of the Club on this occasion. In short, the Company would have ceased to trade, and any liquid assets would have been sold to satisfy creditors. Without funding from Mr Al Jasmi's ownership group, or a change of owner with considerable resources, Wigan Athletic would have been no more. A proud club with 90 years of history, FA Cup winners and Premier League members all within the previous 10 years, would have been consigned to the history books with a devasting impact on the local Wigan community.
Directors Tom Markham and Oliver Gottmann subsequently resigned, issuing a statement via Twitter on 4 June 2023, stating that their position was untenable because Mr Al Jasmi's ownership group's funds had not arrived and they had no visibility on when they would arrive. This left Chairman Talal Al Hammad as the only remaining director and board member of the Club.

On 12 June 2023, following several weeks of discussions, and after closely following developments, HMRC filed a winding-up petition of the Club. The petition would be heard in the High Court on 26 July 2023.

With the backdrop of the impending HMRC and EFL deadlines the Club's Senior Management Team were running the Club on a day-to-day basis under considerable pressure and increased media speculation over the ownership and the future of the Club.

It had now emerged that the Bahraini ownership group was looking to sell Wigan Athletic, with Sarbot Johal, muted as a potential buyer, despite failing to gain previous EFL approval to buy an EFL club.

Concerned about the Club's precarious position, on 9th June 2023, Mike Danson was granted permission to commence due diligence on the Club with a view to purchasing Phoenix 2021 Ltd and clearing its liabilities.

After a swift period of due diligence, and after gaining approval for a change of control from the EFL, an agreement was reached by all parties to transfer ownership of Phoenix 2021 Ltd to Mike Danson, via his company Community Holdco 2023 Ltd, on 14th June 2023.

Upon completion, funds were immediately deposited by Mike, and the very same day, £3.3m was paid in PAYE and wages owed from the previous ownership period. A further significant PAYE payment for June was due a few days later and was also paid by the new ownership group.

HMRC removed the winding up petition on 15 June 2023.

There were many other liabilities that have been taken on by our ownership group that will be payable over the 2023/24 financial year. The Company incurred £2 million in costs for the termination of two Player Management Teams and Executives who left the Club since November 2022. This was due to decisions of the previous ownership group, but the vast majority of this sum is contractually due to be paid in the 2023/24 financial year and will be paid by us.

The football club still has several players signed on contracts by the previous ownership group. Many of these contracts only expire in June 2024 and beyond, thus we are taking on significant costs, and these will take at least twelve months to expire, until we can reduce the losses to more sensible levels.

Off the pitch, the previous ownership group entered into many long term supplier contracts that may hinder our ambitions to improve some areas of the Club in the short term. We have already taken steps to address some of these arrangements and we will continue to make it our priority.

At the conclusion of their contracts, in June 2023, First Team Players Joe Bennett, Steven Caulker, Jordan Cousins, Gwion Edwards, Ryan Nyambe, Tendayi Darikwa, and Jamie Jones departed Wigan Athletic. Jamie McGrath and Jack Whatmough were granted permission to find new clubs. Graham Shinnie also moved on, before the financial year end. Since the year end, Max Power left the club, along with Curtis Tilt, and Will Keane was sold to Preston.

Further sanctions imposed by the EFL following wrongdoings of the previous Ownership Group also include an embargo on our paying transfer fees for players until the Summer of 2024. This EFL sanction has significantly reduced the pool of players available to the club over the last two transfer windows.

One of the first jobs within days of our acquiring control of the Club was to agree new contracts for Charlie Hughes and Thelo Aasgaard who committed their long-term future to the Club. Along with fellow academy graduates Sam Tickle, Scott Smith, Baba Adeeko, Chris Sze, Josh Stones, Harry McHugh and Luke Brennan they have all proved incredibly valuable members of our First Team Squad in League One.

In August 2023, we were delighted to announce that Gregor Rioch was appointed Sporting Director. Although a wide-ranging role, Gregor's main focus will be to ensure a pathway for academy players to the first team, and to oversee player recruitment.

This focus on developing young players as assets is a key strategic priority for the Club. Last season, we had the second oldest squad in the Championship. This season, our starting line-up is usually amongst the youngest three teams across the whole of the EFL.

The support of our FansFund members, and the newly proposed Future Fund will continue to be invaluable in developing our young academy players. All its subscribers should take pride in the contribution they have made towards the development of all our academy players, many of whom are now an integral part of the first team squad.

As I write this report, the First Team finds itself mid-table in League One. This season was always going to be tough, with the aim of retaining our League One status following the eight point deduction and the transfer embargo, whilst trying to reduce costs where possible. However, there have been several impressive and promising performances on the pitch, with highlights being the away victories against Derby County and Bolton Wanderers, and the FA Cup run, culminating in a very special and memorable night hosting Manchester United in front of a sell-out crowd.

We aim to operate a sustainable cost structure, looking to recruit, promote and develop young players, and ultimately trade these assets if offers meet our valuations, enabling the players to progress their careers.
We recognise that we are only the custodians of Wigan Athletic. Our ambition is to ensure that it grows and flourishes, so that in many years time, we can eventually pass it onto the next owner and generation of Latics fans, in a much stronger position both on and off the pitch.

I hope that all supporters and staff can see that any decision made, will be made with the Club's best intentions at its core with a view to ensuring the long-term stability and growth of the Club.

The Directors and Senior Management Team consider that the financial results for the year to 30 June 2023 are disappointing. The significant operating losses were broadly in line with those budgeted, and assurances as to the ability to fund these losses had been given to the EFL by the owner. The loss was increased above budget, due to the unforeseen £2 million in termination costs for player management staff and executives.

Operating losses would have been £17.5 million, save for the write back, of £4.1 million in owner loans, which has been credited to the profit and loss account. £17.5 million would have been the largest annual loss in any filed annual accounts in Wigan Athletic's history.

A financial loss of £13.4 million was recorded for the year.

Income
Turnover for the year was £15.9 million compared to £8.3 million the previous year. This is significantly up on last year due to competing in the Championship v League One. £4.8 million of this came from the Premier League solidarity, with £3.7 million from EFL distributions. Matchday and season ticket sales were £2.6 million, compared to £2.2 million the previous year. £1.6 million was generated from commercial and sponsorship activities, which was £0.5 million higher than a year earlier. The remaining turnover was derived mainly from retail, stadium hire fees, match streaming sales and academy grants.

Administrative Expenses
Total administrative expenditure was £28.7 million compared to £18.0 million the previous year. Administrative expenses would have been £32.8 million without the write back of £4.1 million in owner loans. Salary costs for staff and players increased from £13.0 million last year to £23.2 million this year. This represented 81% of the administrative expenses and was 146% of turnover. Clearly the salary costs were unacceptably high and will be reduced in 2023/24 due to relegation to League One and more prudent management of this area of the business. The remaining expenses relate to the operation of the Football Club, as well as the facilities at the DW Stadium, Christopher Park and Stadium Way Academy sites.

The Directors and Senior Management Team are committed to reducing costs to reasonable levels to ensure that losses remain within the parameters of the EFL financial regulations, and the requirements set by the ownership group.

Player Trading and Investment
During the year the Company generated a profit from the sale of players of £0.4 million, compared to £2.8 million a year earlier. The vast majority of this £0.4 million was due to the activation of various contingent income clauses from players previously sold by the Club. While player trading at times may be unpopular, it is an integral part of the business model that underpins the stability and growth of the Club, and this is a cornerstone of our strategy to build for the future, and to enable investment in fresh new talent.

For reference, Phoenix 2021 Limited retains all the contingent income rights for players sold prior to its period of owning the Club.

Loss for the Period
The Company incurred losses of £13.4 million in the 2022/23 financial year. The Directors and Senior Management Team will seek to reduce this figure going forward, but will be limited by commitments due to player and commercial contracts that will take time to expire.

Future Outlook
The Directors and Senior Management Team aspire to utilise the resources at its disposal at any point in time to achieve the highest possible league position and success in cup competitions for the First Team, whilst at the same time employing prudent financial management to ensure a stable future. The Academy has been a key element of our identity and to our on-field performance and therefore investment into the Academy operations is a crucial element of our strategy.

As part of the long-term player-trading strategy, the transition of Academy players to the First Team will help reduce expenditure on player transfers and create organic growth within our player-trading model, with the intention of increasing the strength and depth of the squad over time. We will invest in player recruitment resources, to ensure that we can attract and retain the best football talent available, within an agreed budget.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
The Company is responsible for continually assessing the risks applicable to the business, with the key risk always being the uncertainty of achieving football success on the field and the consequential significant impact on revenue from central distributions, sponsorship and ticketing.

The Company seeks to reduce and de-risk this uncertainty by:

- Utilising modern player recruitment techniques to attract the best players within our resources;
- Continued investment into the Club's Academy to assist the progression of young talented players to the First Team; and
- Utilising up-to-date analysis and performance measurement tools to assist coaching staff to achieve the best results possible.

Other risks, including financial constraints, commercial and health and safety issues are managed via regular internal management meetings, the use of professional advisors and information and advice received from the Football Association and The EFL. The company works to ensure that every match held at the DW Stadium fully meets all the health and safety criteria for the operation of football grounds.

Financial risk management
The Company is exposed to financial risk through its ability to successfully manage its financial assets and liabilities with the key financial indicator being cash flow: the ability to control a positive cash balance and generate proceeds greater than obligations arising from liabilities as they fall due. The other components of the Company's financial risk are interest rate risk and currency risk.

The Company addresses cash flow risk by carefully managing its working capital inflows and outflows and ensuring, via regular updating of its forecasts, that the necessary facilities are available to finance the working capital position from funding from its ultimate parent company, Community Holdco 2023 Limited.

The Company does not consider that the size of its interest rate exposure warrants the hedging of potential interest rate charges. The Company is exposed to occasional foreign currency exchange risks through its player transactions but does not hedge against this risk because of the infrequency/size of the transactions.

2. ACCOUNTING POLICIES - continued

Player registration fees, signing on fees and agent fees
Transfer fees for player registrations are capitalised as intangible fixed assets and are amortised on a straight line basis over the period of the respective player contracts.

The net book value of player registrations are assessed on an annual basis and any impairment losses arising are recognised in the financial statements in the period in which they arise. Any estimated surpluses arising are not accounted for. The profit or loss arising on the sale of a player registration is calculated after charging any unamortised registration fees and any further fees which become payable as a result of the sale.

Player signing on fees are recognised in the financial statements as salaries and wages in the period during which the payment has been made.

Agents fees are expensed to the financial statements in the accounting period in which they are incurred.

Tangible fixed assets
Depreciation is provided at the following annual rates in order to write off the cost less estimated residual value of each asset over its estimated useful life.
Long leasehold property - over the period of the lease
Fixtures and fittings - 10% on cost
Motor vehicles - 20% on cost
Computer equipment - 20% on cost

Tangible fixed assets are stated at cost less depreciation and any impairment losses.

Impairment of tangible fixed assets
At each reporting date fixed assets are reviewed to determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an impairment loss. If there is an indication of possible impairment, the recoverable amount of any affected asset is estimated and compared with its carrying amount. If the recoverable amount is lower, the carrying amount of the asset is reduced to its recoverable amount and an impairment loss is recognised immediately in the profit or loss.

If an impairment loss is subsequently reversed, the carrying amount of the asset is increased to the revised estimate of its recoverable amount, but not to exceed the amount that would have been determined had no impairment loss been recognised for the asset in prior periods. A reversal of an impairment loss is recognised immediately in the profit or loss.

Stocks
Stocks are valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value, after making due allowance for obsolete and slow moving items.

Financial instruments
Short term financial assets, including trade debtors and debtors arising from player transfers, are measured at transaction price less any impairment.

Short term financial liabilities, including trade creditors and creditors arising from player transfers are measured at transaction price.

Financial assets and liabilities payable after one year are initially measured at fair value and are subsequently measured at amortised cost using the interest rate method.

Taxation
Taxation for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income, except to the extent that it relates to items recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in equity.

Current tax is recognised at the amount of tax payable using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the statement of financial position date.

2. ACCOUNTING POLICIES - continued

Deferred tax
Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not reversed at the statement of financial position date.

Timing differences arise from the inclusion of income and expenses in tax assessments in periods different from those in which they are recognised in financial statements. Deferred tax is measured using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the year end and that are expected to apply to the reversal of the timing difference.

Unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is probable that they will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.

Foreign currencies
Assets and liabilities in foreign currencies are translated into sterling at the rates of exchange ruling at the statement of financial position date. All expenditure is written off as incurred.

18. CONTINGENT LIABILITIES

There are contractual obligations to pay players and management staff dependent on future performance criteria which have not been provided for in these accounts. Performance related payments are recognised in the financial statements in the period in which the criteria are met. The terms of certain contracts with other football clubs in respect of players' transfers include the payment of additional amounts upon fulfilment of specific conditions in the future. Such amounts are not recognised in the financial statements until the specific conditions are fulfilled. The maximum unprovided liability amounts to £178,750.
