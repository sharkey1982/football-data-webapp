STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2025

The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2025.

REVIEW OF BUSINESS
Sporting Results
The team finished in 12th place in EFL League One for the 2024/2025 season and therefore did not achieve our objective of securing a play-off position.

The playing budget available for the first team was sufficient to be in a competitive position for a top six finish. The final points tally of 61 was therefore disappointingly 17 points below the sixth placed team.

The team did occupy a top six place until late January, however a downturn in results saw the team drop into mid table. In March, Head Coach, Darrell Clarke left the Club and was replaced by Conor Hourihane on an interim basis to the end of the season.

In the cup competitions, the team beat Port Vale in the first round of the FA cup, before losing on penalties to Bristol Rovers in the second round.

The team did not progress out of the group stage of the EFL Trophy, losing all three matches.

The team progressed to the third round of the Carabao Cup, beating League One's Wigan Athletic and Championship club Sheffield United to set up a lucrative third round tie against Premier League giants and six-time League Cup winners, Manchester United. This cup run is the key driver of the increased matchday revenue in Note 3 of the accounts.

The Club signed a number of new players in the summer transfer window, including Marc Roberts returning to the Club for a second spell, Georgie Gent joining from Blackburn and Davis Keillor-Dunn joining from Mansfield in the final days of the window. Davis went on to make 44 appearances in all competitions and scoring 19 goals, earning him a place in the EFL League One Team of the Season.

Player sales were below the levels historically achieved by the Club, with Callum Styles' sale to West Bromwich Albion being the only notable sale.

Financial Performance
Turnover of £10.3m represents a 15% increase compared to the previous 13-month period.

Central League distributions increased from £2.6m to £3.1m, driven by the increased EFL basic award following the new Sky Sports broadcast deal.

Match day revenue from match tickets and season tickets increased by £0.8m mainly due to the lucrative progression in the Carabao Cup.

Chief Commercial Officer, Darren Hayes, rejoined the Club for a second spell during the season helping the Club generate over £1m in sponsorship and advertising revenue. This represents a 32% increase compared to the previous 13-month period.

Cost of sales and administrative costs totalled £16.6m compared to £18.6m in the previous 13-month period. This represents a 3.4% pro-rata reduction in operating costs.

This reduction in operating costs comes despite rises in the National Minimum Wage by 9.8% and increases in Employers' National Insurance during the period. Such inflationary increases added significant pressure to the Club's cost base. These changes affected not only staff paid at minimum wage, but also a wide range of other matchday and non-matchday operational costs.

Due to the increase in turnover and reduction of costs, the operating loss incurred of £6.3m reflects a £3.3m improvement compared to the previous 13-month period.

As referred to previously, the level of profitable player trading in the year did not match that of the 2023/2024 season. A £7m variance compared to the previous season is a key factor in reporting an overall loss of the season of £6.6m compared to £2.8m in the previous season.

Cashflow Statement
The amount of cash needed to fund operating activities has decreased slightly from £9.2m in 2023/2024 to £8.7m in 2024/2025.

This shortfall of operating cash required to run the Club has been funded by equity raises from subscribing shareholders of £5.4m (2024 : £4.2m).

Cash of £4.9m (2024 : £5.2m) was also received from the sale of players in previous seasons, where the payments are spread over multiple years.

The Club also spent £0.9m of cash (2024 : £0.8m) on incoming player transfers. Again, this partially relates to players brought in during previous seasons, but paid for in instalments.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
The principal risks and uncertainties facing the Club are:
- The material impact on revenue dependent on divisional status.
- Cash management within the constraints of available working capital.
- A lack of influence on the principal revenue streams relating to central broadcasting contracts.
- Increasing costs of energy, food, fuel and labour costs, affecting both the Club's available resources and the economic position of supporters and partners locally and nationally.

Based on these forecasts the Club can meet its liabilities as they fall due and the Directors have concluded that it is appropriate for the financial statements to be prepared on a going concern basis.

2. ACCOUNTING POLICIES - continued

Significant judgements and estimates
In the application of the company's accounting policies, the directors are required to make judgements, estimates and assumptions about the carrying amount of assets and liabilities that are not readily apparent from other sources. The estimates and associated assumptions are based on historical experience and other factors that are considered to be relevant. Actual results may differ from these estimates.

The estimates and underlying assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting estimates are recognised in the period in which the estimate is revised where the revision only affects that period, or in the period of the revision and future periods where the revision affects both current and future periods.

The directors do not believe there are any material judgements or sources of estimation uncertainty that require disclosure.

Turnover
Turnover comprises income receivable from football and related commercial activities, exclusive of VAT.

Gate receipts and other match day revenue are recognised as the games are played.

Central distributions and solidarity payments from the Football League and the Premier League are recognised evenly over the course of the financial year.

Revenue derived from season tickets is credited to income in the period to which it related. Amounts received in advance are credited to accruals and deferred income in the Balance Sheet.

Sponsorship, advertising and similar commercial income is recognised over the duration of the respective contracts. Amounts received in advance are credited to accruals and deferred income in the Balance Sheet.

Players' registrations
The costs associated with the acquisition of players' registrations are capitalised as intangible fixed assets. These costs are amortised in equal annual instalments over the period of the respective players' contract. Players' registrations are written down for impairment when the carrying amount exceeds the amount recoverable through use or sale. Any transfer fees payable as a result of the occurrence of one or more uncertain future events are capitalised when it is probable such event will occur.

Tangible fixed assets
Tangible fixed assets are stated at cost or valuation less accumulated depreciation. Cost includes costs directly attributable to making the asset capable of operating as intended.

Stocks

Taxation

Deferred tax

21. EMPLOYEE BENEFIT OBLIGATIONS

The company contributes to the Football League Limited Pension and Life Assurance Scheme, a defined benefit pension scheme operated on behalf of all league clubs. Under section 28 of FRS 102 the scheme would be treated as defined multi-employer benefit scheme. The assets of the scheme are held separately from those of the company, being invested with insurance companies. Sufficient information is not available for full disclosure.

Following a review of the Minimum Funding Requirement of the scheme, accrual of benefits of the final salary section of the scheme was suspended as at 31 August 1999. In light of the exceptional circumstances affecting the scheme, the trustees of the scheme commissioned an independent actuary's report on the Minimum Funding Requirement position and a substantial deficit was identified. Under the pensions act 1985, participating employers will be required to contribute to the deficit.

The latest actuarial valuation of the scheme was carried out as at 1 September 2023, as such the charge to the profit and loss in the period was £30,007 (2024: £31,483). The deficit was being repaid in monthly instalments of £2,511 from 1 September 2024, increasing to £2,637 per month from 1 September 2025. Monthly repayments are due to increase by a further 5% p.a from 1 September 2026.
