Football Performance

We introduced a new football operations structure in October 2022 with the appointment of Rich Hughes as Sporting Director and the subsequent appointment of John Mousinho as Head Coach in January 2023.

 

With it, we adopted a new football recruitment strategy, targeting the permanent acquisition of younger progressive players with the ability to improve the squad and using loan signings as a supplement to squad planning. We had a busy pre-season signing a total of fourteen players with seven out-of-contract players Will Norris, Ryan Schofield, Jack Sparkes, Regan Poole, Connor Shaughnessy, Ben Stevenson, Gavin Whyte and four permanent players signings Anthony Scully, Kusini Yengi, Christian Saydee and Terry Devlin for transfer fees some of which were completed before the previous financial year end of June 2023.

 

In addition, the club made three loan transfers during the pre-season Transfer Window of Alex Robertson, Abu Kamara and Tino Anjorin. Post the summer Transfer Window, we also signed out of contract Josh Martin to support the squad.

 

We started August unbeaten, with nine points from the first five games followed by a six-game winning run starting at Barnsley in September which took us to the top of the league. We subsequently fell to second position in late November after our first loss of the season at home to Blackpool which was memorable for the fantastic support from our supporters despite a 0-4 home loss. We managed to get back to the summit by winning at Northampton on 2 December and subsequently never let go of first place for the rest of a memorable season.

 

This success was achieved despite suffering significant injuries throughout the season to key players such as Regan Poole, Joe Morrell, Terry Devlin and Alex Robertson all to season-ending injuries. After a difficult period of games over the Christmas and New Year period we re-bolstered the squad in the January Transfer Window with the signing of Myles Peart Harris (loan), out-of-contract Matt Macey and permanent signings for transfer fees of Callum Lang, Owen Moxon and Tom McIntyre. Post the January Transfer Window, we also signed out of contract Lee Evans to support the squad.

 

There were some unforgettable matches at Fratton Park throughout the season with the home games against Bolton Wanderers, Oxford United and Derby County being stand-out atmospheres and we achieved dramatic late wins against Wycombe Wanderers and Carlisle United. We sealed promotion with another memorable late win at home against Barnsley on Tuesday 16 April and we completed the season with a win away at Lincoln City with a total of ninety-seven points.

 

The next day we celebrated a double title triumph with a special event on Southsea Common which saw fans, owners, club officials and players from both our successful men’s and women’s teams come together to mark a memorable campaign in which both teams won their respective titles.

 

We were knocked out of the FA Cup in the first round away at Chesterfield and in the EFL Cup we lost to Peterborough in the second round. We went out of the EFL Trophy at the last thirty-two stage at home to AFC Wimbledon.

 

An amazing season on the pitch was recognised with a string of awards including John Mousinho voted EFL Manager of the Year and Andrew Cullen winning overall Chief Executive of the Year Award at the Football Business Awards ceremony in London.

Academy

The Academy continued to operate with a staff structure above EPPP Category 3 regulatory requirements which was evident by another strong annual audit performance with the Premier League Audit Company.

 

The financial running cost of the Academy rose in line with national minimum wage increases and the increased costs for house parents to support 11 of our 18 scholars living away from home. The increased games programme volume required continued pitch rentals and there was further investment in our staff development and hardware in our sport science department.

 

In July 2024 the Academy had it first player sales in five seasons with two players moving to Premier League Clubs for agreed training compensation. No professional contracts awarded to academy scholars in the 2023-24 season which in part was impacted by our promotion to EFL Championship albeit we remain confident of both the progression of our players across all age groups and the pathway which supports such progression. Our teams have consistently competed against higher category academies and performed well as we stretch the development of the players.

 

We took one Foundation Phase team to a UK based tournament during the season and this allowed players and staff to gain the experience of knock-out football and for the young players to stay away from home. We reached the final of this tournament which demonstrated our competitiveness against other professional clubs. In the Youth Development Phase, we also took a U13 squad away to an overseas tournament in Madeira which was our first such overseas tournament participation in four years.

 

We increased our engagement with local grass roots clubs and community via provision of free coaching seminars for local football coaches to promote relationships within our local network. We also worked closely with Sophie’s Legacy (our selected academy charity partner for the season) which offers support to families with children in hospital on a long-term basis with staff and U18 players taking part in promotional events including a charity run, hospital visits and pizza delivery to parents and kids on the hospital wards.

 

Pompey Women

In June 2023, Portsmouth Women were fully integrated into the club, alongside the men’s team under the ownership of The Tornante Company via a new separate subsidiary company and allowing semi-professional contracts to be offered to the players for the first time.

One of the overarching goals behind this change was the promotion of football, sport and healthy lifestyles to women and young girls across Portsmouth and a fitting legacy from the 125 Anniversary programme.

In 2023-24 Pompey Women won the National League South and with it promotion to the Women's Championship.

Portsmouth Football Club 125th Anniversary Celebrations

In April 2023 we celebrated our 125th anniversary marking a significant milestone in the club's illustrious history and throughout the year there was a comprehensive program of events to honour the milestone celebrating our local community and inspiring future generations.

 

A dedicated ‘125 Committee’ was established to spearhead a year-long program of events to commemorate the occasion. The committee was chaired by Colin Farmery and comprised our key club stakeholders being Pompey Supporters' Trust, Pompey History Society, Pompey Women, Tony Goodall Fans’ Conference and the University of Portsmouth.

 

Highlights included the unveiling of a statue honouring club legend Jimmy Dickinson, street art celebrating other Pompey greats, events with cultural significance such as artistic exhibitions and a commemorative friendly against the Royal Artillery.

Fratton Park Stadium Development

When the new season commenced in August 2023, we were on the final leg of the Fratton Park development project. In 2021, we adopted a major stadium works programme to address long-standing health and safety requirements which had been hanging over the club for many decades.

 

The development work in the Milton End was completed in December 2023 thereby bringing stadium capacity back over 20,000 for the first time in many years. We achieved our first 20,000 plus attendance in thirteen years on 17 February 2024 for the Reading home game and thereafter going on to record four more 20,000-plus attendances in the season.

 

The stadium project was a big team effort amongst many different stakeholders who all contributed towards ensuring that whilst incorporating the provision of new facilities for our supporters; we retained the traditional close-knit atmosphere and historic heritage of Fratton Park.

 

Portsmouth City Council had originally ensured that a significant Escrow sum had been separately allocated towards stadium works as part of the financial agreement in which the club emerged from administration back in 2013 and later a multi-year works programme was agreed with the Council which first got under way in 2016/17.

 

PMC Construction – then as now a main club sponsor –have played a major role in addressing the many quirks of our historic building and have been inspirational in bringing everything together working in conjunction with key contractors Arena Seating, HGP Architects, Hampshire Electrical , Matrix IT and many others. As a club, we will always be indebted to Steve Cripps for his absolute commitment to restoring Fratton Park to a vibrant and much-loved stadium.

 

Michael Eisner and his family were immediately attracted to the traditions and history of Fratton Park when they first visited the stadium in 2017. Michael played a major role in blending the tradition of the ground into the new construction with his own US architect, Bob Stern. They both played a big role weaving our heritage within the design work of the development work and restoration of the Archibald Leitch truss in the South Stand.

 

This was not a linear process and involved numerous visits to other stadiums including Fulham FC and the London Stadium to review how the steel framed lower sections (a system used on our lower South Stand) had been incorporated in similar facilities. Another other core requirement from the works project was the provision of additional facilities and concessions which is reflected in the transformation of the Milton End.

 

We owe a huge thank you to all our various supporter working parties including the Heritage & Advisory Board, the Matchday Experience Working Groups, the North Stand and South Stand Supporter Working Groups and Tony Goodall Fans Conference, who have all greatly contributed to the direction, shape and final design of the works.

 

We committed to further stadium infrastructure works at the end of the season with the addition of a new South Stand TV gantry which was an extremely challenging engineering task given the lack of space and tightness to the pitch. Other summer project work saw the First Team and Academy move into new office, gym, analysis and medical facilities in the main building at the training ground purchased three years earlier. We also created a new North Stand mezzanine with new bars and toilets, while additional toilets were constructed in the home areas of the Milton End.

 

We owe a big thank you to our owners Tornante for supporting this stadium development over recent years to put in place long-term foundations to benefit the club for many years moving forward. At the end of the financial year 2024, we had spent £19m on Fratton Park and stadium development works since Tornante acquired the club in 2017.

Pompey Health and Fitness Club & Training Ground

We continued infrastructure work on our Training Ground facilities which saw the integration of first team and academy facilities into the main building and continued work on the PHFC gym facility.

 

Revenue from Pompey Health Fitness and Club was £1.3 million and an overall net loss of £0.1 million which was impacted by inflation across the cost base including labour and energy costs.

 

Trading Performance

Our stadium capacity was restricted until completion of the Milton End in December and the increased capacity to 21,000 enabled us to boost our average attendance to 18,952 being an uplift of circa 1,000 from the previous financial year. In the second half of the season, we capped our Season Tickets to 15,000 and introduced a season ticket waiting list at Fratton Park for the first time in many years.

 

We had record Hospitality kiosk sales from our partnership with Piglets Pantry which was enhanced by the investment in new concession facilities as part of the stadium development. The fan zone behind the Fratton End continued to thrive which created a hub of pre-match atmosphere and events assisted by our Matchday Experience Working Groups and providing children’s activities, with music from a DJ and new food and drink facilities. Merchandising sales also reached record sales with strong demand for all three shirts supplied from our partners, Nike Just Sport. A 125 Anniversary merchandising range also proved to be extremely popular amongst supporters of all ages.

 

We incurred a net profit of £34,000 on the transfer of players during the year. The traditional ‘EBITDA’ (Earnings before Interest Depreciation & Amortisation) showed a £3.8 million loss. The depreciation and amortisation charges of £1.8 million represent non-cash write-downs, which include depreciation on assets acquired when the club came out of administration, and £913,000 in respect of the write-down of intangible player assets.

 

This loss above has been affected by a number of non-recurring items such as contingent payments on player transfer agreements and bonus payments to players and staff all triggered by promotion to EFL Championship.

Balance Sheet

The loss in the year reduced the Balance Sheet to a total of £26.5 million net assets with net current liabilities of £3.5 million. It should be noted that included within creditors due less than one year on the balance sheet, is £5.3 million of deferred income in relation to advance season ticket, hospitality and commercial income which will be released to the profit and loss as games are played during the 2024/25 season and hence will not require repayment.

 

During the year we invested £0.9m on the renovation and improvement works at the Training Ground and gym facilities and a further £4.1 million on Fratton Park development work to put in place foundations for the future long-term benefit of the club.

 

In terms of cash flows, the club recorded a net cash outflow from operating activities before working capital adjustments of £3.8 million for the year ended 30th June 2024.

 

We remain debt free.

 

Ownership

The club drew down further funds from the Owners, Tornante, during the year which was converted into share capital which stood at £37 million in June 2024.

Financial risk management objectives and policies

Principal risks and uncertainties

The principal risk to the business is the performance of the team which may affect revenue obtained from games and sponsorship. The cost base of the business, in common with other football clubs, is relatively fixed in the short-term, hence unfavourable movements in revenue can lead to a significant variation in profits.

 

The company is aware of this risk and addresses the issue by seeking to strengthen the squad through the development and acquisition of players. It is the aim of the directors to maximise the flexibility of the cost base to deal with unexpected revenue reductions.

 

Financial risk management

The directors have reviewed the financial risk management objectives and policies of the company. They do not believe there to be significant risks in this area. The company does not enter any hedging instruments as there are not believed to be any material exposures. It does not enter into any financial instruments for speculative purposes.

 

Appropriate trade terms are negotiated with suppliers and customers. Management review these terms, the relationships with suppliers and customers and manages any exposure on normal trade terms. The company prepares regular forecasts of cash flow and liquidity, with which the directors assess the ongoing cash commitments of the company as part of a strict cash flow management programme.
