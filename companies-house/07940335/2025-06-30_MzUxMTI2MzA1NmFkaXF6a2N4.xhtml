Football Performance

After the success in winning the League 1 title in 2024, we returned to the Championship for the first time since 2011-12.

 

We had a busy pre-season signing five out-of-contract players - Josh Murphy, Jordan Williams, Andre Dozzell, Matt Ritchie, Jordan Archer and six permanent players signed for transfer fees; Abdoulaye Kamara, Elias Sorensen, Ibane Bowat, Nicolas Schmid, Harvey Blair and Jacob Farrell.

 

In addition, the club completed three loan signings during the summer transfer window - Freddie Potts, Mark O’Mahony and Sammy Silvera.

 

We were provided with a testing start to our new Championship campaign with early fixtures against many teams recently in the Premier League. Leeds United, Luton Town, Burnley and Sheffield United were amongst four of our first seven opponents. That challenge then intensified with several injuries to key players. Despite some good performances we struggled to get an elusive first victory until we won at Queens Park Rangers in October, to take us off the bottom of the table.

 

The unexpected early return of Colby Bishop in November after summer heart surgery provided a significant morale boost to both the team and supporters with Colby’s return marked as a goal-scoring substitute in our home game against Preston North End. This also marked a change in form and we subsequently amassed 13 wins and 44 points from our remaining 31 matches. After occupying a bottom three league placing for the early part of the season, we escaped the drop zone with victory at home to Middlesbrough in mid-January and went on to maintain our Championship league status with two games to spare, with a victory against Watford on Easter Monday.

 

We added to the squad in the January transfer window with the loan signings of Rob Atkinson, Isaac Hayden, Adil Aouchiche, Kaide Gordon; and permanent signings for transfer fees of Hayden Matthews, Thomas Waddingham, Cohen Bramall and Ben Killip. Following the closure of the January transfer window, we signed out of contract Alex Milosevic to provide further defensive cover.

 

We exited the third round of the FA Cup at Wycombe Wanderers. In the EFL Cup, we were defeated in the first round by Millwall.

Academy

The Academy has continued to focus on player recruitment and development and whilst the development cycle can take several years, we are beginning to see evidence of progress.

 

Harry Clout made an FA Cup debut and became the first U18 player since the 2021-22 season to complete a debut in both league and cup competitions and Harry was offered a full professional contract at the end of the season.

 

We have invested an incredible amount of energy and time in enhancing the governance of the Academy wide operation. This has involved the creation and implementation of a multi-year strategic plan, stretching departmental KPI’s and a coaching and off-field player support programme that is enabling our players to be supported and stretched at the right moments.

 

This approach to the Academy programme was recognised with high praise when we received the results from our first full audit in four seasons from the Premier League audit company. The fantastic work by the staff over those four seasons enabled us to move all seven audit categories to at least the second highest level of ‘Good’ which was a real milestone and one that offers us the foundations to continue the development of the academy performance.

 

Off-field success has continued with our scholars achieving notable results in their educational courses, and a number of them successfully achieving A level passes.

 

The U18s players and staff also remain highly committed to supporting a local charity of choice each season, with time and support given on multiple occasions to help the charity carry out its incredible work.

 

Throughout the continual blended games programme that we offer, player development and player enjoyment has been evident. This staff observation was confirmed at end of season surveys with players and parents, and we believe the Academy has once again made progress over the season.

 

Portsmouth Women

After winning promotion from the FAWNL Southern Division title in May 2024, the team turned fully professional in July 2024 and in time to commence their campaign in the second division of the newly constituted Barclays Women’s Super League.

 

It proved to be a tough start with the team taking just two points prior to Christmas and not winning a Championship game until March when they defeated fellow strugglers, Sheffield United at Bramall Lane. The team completed a “great escape” taking eleven points from their final six games to eventually finish six points clear of the relegation places.

 

In the FA Cup, Pompey Women defeated Luton Town in Round 3 and Blackburn Rovers in Round 4 before exiting in Round 5 after defeat by Sunderland.

 

In July 2025, the team moved its training base to Pompey in the Community’s John Jenkins Stadium at Moneyfields. The club played three matches in 2024-25 at Fratton Park with the goal to play all matches at Fratton Park, once a new hybrid grass pitch is laid.

 

Whilst the core ambition remains continuity of representation in the top two divisions of women’s professional football, we continue to strive to use the team and players to inspire and influence the promotion of football, sport, and healthy lifestyles amongst women and young girls across Portsmouth.

Fan Engagement 2024-25

Portsmouth Football Club’s Fan Engagement approach seeks to continually improve the club’s dialogue with supporters and ensure that their voices are heard and considered in decision-making processes that affect our fans. We value the input of our supporters into decisions that affect them most and are committed to creating a strong and positive relationships. Our approach is to deliver inclusivity and transparency.

 

To achieve that goal, the club works to engage supporters in a number of ways. We meet regularly with our official supporters’ clubs including Central Branch, Armed Forces, Chichester, Isle of Wight, London, South-West, South Wales and Northern Blues throughout the season. We also meet with Portsmouth FC Women’s Supporter Club which was formed last year and had three active meetings with club guests in 2024-2025.

 

Additionally, we continue to operate regular meetings per season with each of the following: The Tony Goodall Fans’ Conference, Heritage & Advisory Board, Matchday Experience and Ticketing groups – all set up with the purpose of interacting across different areas of the club.

 

All these measures ensure we can engage, listen and learn from supporters on the areas that matter most to them. A list of our recognised supporter groups can be found on our website. We operate a multi-level engagement policy, over and above our fan group meetings. We held a fans’ forum broadcast live on BBC Radio Solent at the beginning of the season to allow all those interested in attending to listen to, contribute and engage with a number of club personnel, including the CEO, the club captain and the first team head coaches of both the men’s and women’s teams.

 

We publish all minutes and outputs from our meetings of the Tony Goodall Fans’ Conference on the club website to ensure all supporters are aware of the topics and discussions. Our Supporter Liaison Officer, Johnny Moore, also provides periodical updates for all our fans on the progress made against our Fan Engagement objectives.

 

The Tony Goodall Fans’ Conference meetings throughout the season were attended by senior staff, including Chief Executive, Andrew Cullen; Chief Operating Officer, Tony Brown; Sporting Director, Rich Hughes and Chief Commercial Officer, Mark Judges.

 

Club Chairman, Michael Eisner attended all four Heritage & Advisory Board meetings, with Director, Eric Eisner. The CEO, Head Coach and Sporting Director attended the Pompey Supporters’ Trust AGM in November 2024, with Michael Eisner and Eric Eisner sending a video message to the same AGM.

 

Interviews throughout the season are given by CEO Andrew Cullen to the wider fan-base through local media, including The Portsmouth News and BBC Radio Solent. The CEO’s matchday programme notes are also publicised on the official club website.

 

Surveys throughout the season regarding travel modes to games and the matchday experience were conducted and will continue.

 

All complaints and enquiries to the club’s ‘info’ email and official website are monitored by the Supporter Liaison Officer, with a five working day requirement for acknowledgement or reply by the SLO or the relevant member of staff. There is an updated and full complaints policy in the 2025-26 club customer charter.

 

In December 2024 the club was proud along with members from the PST and the Supporter Chair of the Tony Goodall Fans Conference to receive the Supporter Engagement Award at the Football Supporters Association (FSA) Awards. The club was also short listed for the EFL Fan Engagement Award in April.

Fratton Park Stadium Development

After completing the Fratton Park development project in 2024 and bringing stadium capacity back to over 20,000, we continued the stadium work with a number of major projects in the close season including a new South Stand TV Gantry, North Stand mezzanines, additional bathrooms in the Milton End, a refurbished Victory Suite to develop our non-matchday business and enhanced the Fan Zone.

 

The addition of a new South Stand TV gantry was an extremely challenging engineering task given the lack of space and tightness to the pitch. We were indebted to Steve Haskins our long-standing club engineer and NJS Scaffolding, G&S Fabrications and Steve Cripps and all his team at PMC.

 

The new North Stand mezzanine improved supporter facilities with new bars and toilets, while additional toilets were also constructed in the home areas of the Milton End.

 

A new TV studio was created in the south corner of the Milton End and has quickly been established as the home of Pompey+, a new matchday show hosted by George Wedlake, with guest appearances from regular former players as guests. This has all helped to grow our overseas digital subscriber base.

 

The safety to supporters attending Fratton Park was further improved in summer 2025 with the installation of an independent water sprinkler system and tank. This will add resilience should a fire break out and simultaneously, the city’s water mains pressure fail.

 

Pompey Health and Fitness Club & Training Ground

Revenue from Pompey Health and Fitness Club continued to deliver growth with stronger yields from membership subscriptions. We are grateful to our health club partner, Mosaic who provide excellence and professionalism to the operation of Pompey Health and Fitness Club.

 

The training ground saw further improvements to the pitches in the close season. The former first team building to the south of the training ground was gutted and refurbished to create a standalone Academy Hub with dressing rooms for home and away teams and match officials. New offices, common room for the scholars, medical rooms, safeguarding rooms and an analysis suite were all added. This move enabled the extension of the first team dressing room within the main building, a new and larger dressing room area for first team staff, as well as a new reception area to greet visitors to the training ground.

 

Trading Performance

Our new increased stadium capacity and return to the Championship enabled us to boost our average league attendance to 20,263, an uplift of 7% on the average from the previous season. We capped season tickets at 15,000 and maintained a waiting list for over 3,000 prospective season ticket holders. We thank our incredible supporters, not only for their passion and support which played a huge part in the consolidation of our hard-earned divisional status but which also delivered the best stadium occupancy rate in the Championship.

 

Piglets Pantry delivered another year of kiosk record-breaking sales, supported by the new extended upper mezzanine in the North Stand, higher attendances from visiting supporters and the enhanced Fan Zone and Victory Suite. We thank the Piglets ownership and all their staff for their passionate and continued commitment to delivery, quality and excellence.

Financial Risk Management Objectives and Policies

 

Principal Risks and Uncertainties

The principal risk to the business is the performance of the team which may affect revenue obtained from games and sponsorship. The cost base of the business, in common with other football clubs, is relatively fixed in the short-term, hence unfavourable movements in revenue can lead to a significant variation in profits.

 

The company is aware of this risk and addresses the issue by seeking to strengthen the squad through the development and acquisition of players. It is the aim of the directors to maximise the flexibility of the cost base to deal with unexpected revenue reductions.

 

Financial risk management

The directors have reviewed the financial risk management objectives and policies of the company. They do not believe there to be significant risks in this area. The company does not enter any hedging instruments as there are not believed to be any material exposures. It does not enter any financial instruments for speculative purposes.

Portsmouth Football Club Carbon Footprint Statement

The club continued its commitment to developing to improving sustainability and environmental awareness throughout the club across our properties at Fratton Park, Pompey Health and Fitness Club and the Training Ground.

 

A club wide working environmentally sustainable Working Group, established in 2023 under the leadership of the CEO, continues to meet with departments across the club represented on this working group. Our external operating partners from Pompey in the Community, Piglets Pantry, Pompey Health and Fitness Club and Just Sport are also represented on this Working Group.

 

In summer 2025, the club was awarded EFL Green Club ‘Silver’ status. EFL Green Clubs is a League-wide voluntary scheme, delivered in partnership with GreenCode, supporting clubs to improve their environmental practices and operations.

 

This award recognised the club’s concentrated effort to implement environmentally sustainable practices across its properties.

 

This includes creating green spaces to help develop biodiverse areas and encourage wildlife; working towards an annual reduction in energy consumption; providing a number of vegan options for players and staff at the training ground and at the stadium on a matchday; offering a range of sustainable travel options for staff and visitors, with EV charging points set to be installed; working with local suppliers to reduce our environmental impact; water saving measures being put in place, with stewards and the academy provided with reusable water bottles and water fountains being installed; and waste management measures improved, with different bins provided for supporters and Piglet’s Pantry donating unused matchday food to charity groups.

 

We recognise that Portsmouth is an island city – one situated within a low-lying coastal environment and at acute risk from climate change. The football club is at the heart of the city, with a privileged opportunity to take a lead in developing a culture with suppliers, supporters, the community and employees, where environmental sustainability becomes central to everything we do.
