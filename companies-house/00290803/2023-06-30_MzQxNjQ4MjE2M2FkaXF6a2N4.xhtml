The directors take a cautious view with respect to financial risk management with the company being well managed by its board who operate a strict and prudent fiscal policy in all aspects.

 

Budgets and projections indicate that revenue generated from footballing activities being season ticket sales, gate receipts, FL Club distributions, competition prize money and TV & broadcasting revenue are insufficient to cover expected costs.

Principal risks and uncertainties

 

The nature of professional football presents many risks and uncertainties. The Board is aware of the risks that affect the company and they are regularly reviewed.

Liquidity risk is the main financial risk faced by the company and this is addressed by the careful management of its working capital requirements. Uncertainties connected with the various income streams are acknowledged and assessed and future funding requirements are continually reviewed by the board. The board are aware of the significant uncertainties that potentially impact the company, further information on these uncertainties can be found in the going concern section within note 1.2 to the financial statements.

Review of the business

The team adjusted to life back in League One with four out of five league wins and hopes were high for a quick return to the Championship. However, we were unable to maintain that run and four straight league defeats plus 2 in cup competitions dampened expectations. Consistency was difficult to find and with more disappointing results in December and over the Christmas period culminating in a 0-3 home defeat to Wycombe on New Year’s Day, the club parted company with club legend Grant McCann and his assistant Cliff Byrne.

Review of the business (continued)

With the club in eighth position and five points off the playoffs, Darren Ferguson returned for his fourth spell agreeing to manage the team for the rest of the season. As Club Chairman and owner Darragh MacAnthony said ‘When you look for a manager in January, it is a very difficult time, an impossible time to get someone in, so if you were to write down what you would want in a manager, someone who identifies with how you work as a football club, someone who has had multiple promotions on their CV, someone with a style of football that gets people off their seats, where would you find a manager like that?' It just so happens we have one that lives nearby and has agreed to come and help us until the end of the season.

Results improved and with 13 wins out of 19, what looked like an improbable play off place earlier in the season was sealed with a fantastic win on the last day of scheduled fixtures at Barnsley.

Buoyed by a full house at the Weston Homes Stadium, supporters were treated to a spectacular first leg play off semi-final display against Sheffield Wednesday resulting in a 4-0 win. We all know what happened in the now infamous second leg at Hillsborough, and the utter despair of being a few seconds away from Wembley, only for the Owls to score in the 90th minute to take the tie to extra time. Sadly, with the tie all square at 5-5, our dreams of Wembley and promotion back to the Championship were shattered in the penalty shootout.

The club continued to invest heavily in the Academy, and we completed our first full season as a Category 2 Academy. The U18’s had a great season finishing 3rd in the league and winning two cups, the Professional Development League Cup, and the David Joyce County Cup. Benji Arthur received an England call up and featured in the England U18s International tournament in Lisbon. Expectations remain high for a number of players in the Academy programme, and during the season there were over 100 first team league appearances for academy graduates.

The Posh Women continue to thrive, and another successful season followed a move from the Idverde Training Ground to a new home, The Abbey Lawn, at Bourne Town FC which enabled them to attract many new supporters. A top four finish in the FA Women’s National League Division One, reaching the third round of the Vitality Women’s FA Cup and winning the Northants FA County Cup gave a record-breaking season on the pitch.

The club successfully applied for and received a 3-year license from the FA to set up a girls Emerging Talent Centre (ETC) in September 2022. With four age groups – U10s, U12s, U14s and U16s this provides the opportunity for young girls in the community to play football, receive high quality coaching and a pathway into the senior ladies’ football teams.

It would be remiss not to mention the fantastic team behind the scenes. The success of all of Peterborough United’s football teams would not be possible without their hard work, commitment, and dedication.

Finally, as always, we would like to thank our superb fans, sponsors, and partners for their continued support.

Many faces, who had served us well and delivered promotion in the COVID 20-21 season, departed over the summer and we wish them all well with their new clubs. We returned for the new season, refreshed with the youngest team in the league, and the rebuild began. Some scintillating performances have been delivered and at the time of writing we are sitting 5th in the table and have the Bristol Street Motors Cup Final at Wembley to look forward to. Our first visit to the national stadium in ten years.

Going concern and post balance sheet events

The Board of Directors are required to prepare the statutory financial statements on the going concern basis unless it is inappropriate to presume that the company will continue in business.true

The company has net liabilities of £15.9m (2022: £12.4m) and made a loss after tax in the current year of £3.5m (2022: loss of £0.9m) and has net current liabilities of £12.5m (2022: £13.3m).

Day to day working capital requirements are met through existing cash and banking facilities, bank loans, investor financing and from the support of its beneficial owners by way of shareholder related loans.

To enable the Board to assess the adequacy of the financial facilities available to the company, they have used management information, including a recent review of forecasts which extend beyond one year from the date of approval of these financial statements.

The forecasts make key assumptions around season ticket sales and matchday income, football performance, overhead costs, future player sales, contingent receipts and transfer fees payable and the company’s ability to secure future debt and/or equity finance. Any reliance placed on the forecasts by the Board is dependent upon the above factors. At the time of preparation, the Board recognises that current projections indicate that the company will require additional funding within the next 12 months to meet its operational cash flow requirements and to facilitate loan repayments and cash requirements for other financing commitments. The assumptions made by the Board when determining the expected funding requirement do not include any contingent income relating to potential sell-on fees for Ivan Toney.

The amount of additional funding required is uncertain and the Board acknowledges that the required amounts will depend upon significant uncertainties relating to:

-the assumptions relating to the timing and amounts expected to be received in respect of player sales;

-the ongoing support of the beneficial owner, Darragh MacAnthony.

On the basis of the above, the Board acknowledges the existence of material uncertainties which may cast significant doubt over the company’s ability to continue as a going concern.

The Board have obtained a letter of support from the ultimate beneficial owner, Darragh MacAnthony, confirming the intention to support the company and to provide additional loan facilities as required to meet any shortfalls that may arise from variances to the forecast cash flows. In addition, further confirmations have been provided of the intention to support the company by not demanding repayment of loans owing to Darragh MacAnthony for the foreseeable future. The level and timing of the potential cash requirements have been considered by the Board, however, they acknowledge that the letter of support is not legally binding and there is no contractual certainty that the shareholder will make available such funding, nor require repayment of the existing loans. If the support of the shareholder was required and not forthcoming, the company may not be able to raise additional funding to provide sufficient cash to enable it to meet its liabilities as they fall due. This creates a material uncertainty which may cast significant doubt over the company’s ability to continue as a going concern.

Nevertheless, on the basis of the information available to the Board at the current time, the Board believe that there is a reasonable expectation that the company will have adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. Accordingly, they continue to adopt the going concern basis of preparation in these financial statements.

1.3
Turnover

Turnover represents amounts receivable for net gate and ticket receipts, box subscriptions, television and sponsorship revenue, FA and prize money, contingent transfer fees, player loan fees, rental income and other commercial activities net of VAT. 1.8
Financial instruments

The company has elected to apply the provisions of Section 11 ‘Basic Financial Instruments’ and Section 12 ‘Other Financial Instruments Issues’ of FRS 102 to all of its financial instruments.

 

Financial instruments are recognised in the company's balance sheet when the company becomes party to the contractual provisions of the instrument.

 

Financial assets and liabilities are offset, with the net amounts presented in the financial statements, when there is a legally enforceable right to set off the recognised amounts and there is an intention to settle on a net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.

Basic financial assets

Basic financial assets, which include debtors and cash and bank balances, are initially measured at transaction price including transaction costs and are subsequently carried at amortised cost using the effective interest method unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is measured at the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest. Financial assets classified as receivable within one year are not amortised.

Other financial assets

Other financial assets, including investments in equity instruments which are not subsidiaries, associates or joint ventures, are initially measured at fair value, which is normally the transaction price. Such assets are subsequently carried at fair value and the changes in fair value are recognised in profit or loss, except that investments in equity instruments that are not publicly traded and whose fair values cannot be measured reliably are measured at cost less impairment.

Impairment of financial assets

Financial assets, other than those held at fair value through profit and loss, are assessed for indicators of impairment at each reporting end date.

 

Financial assets are impaired where there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events that occurred after the initial recognition of the financial asset, the estimated future cash flows have been affected. If an asset is impaired, the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset’s original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss.

 

If there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not exceed what the carrying amount would have been, had the impairment not previously been recognised. The impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.

Derecognition of financial assets

Financial assets are derecognised only when the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset expire or are settled, or when the company transfers the financial asset and substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to another entity, or if some significant risks and rewards of ownership are retained but control of the asset has transferred to another party that is able to sell the asset in its entirety to an unrelated third party.

Classification of financial liabilities

Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of the company after deducting all of its liabilities.

Deferred tax

Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised for all timing differences and deferred tax assets are recognised to the extent that it is probable that they will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits. Such assets and liabilities are not recognised if the timing difference arises from goodwill or from the initial recognition of other assets and liabilities in a transaction that affects neither the tax profit nor the accounting profit.

 

The carrying amount of deferred tax assets is reviewed at each reporting end date and reduced to the extent that it is no longer probable that sufficient taxable profits will be available to allow all or part of the asset to be recovered. Deferred tax is calculated at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the period when the liability is settled or the asset is realised. Deferred tax is charged or credited in the profit and loss account, except when it relates to items charged or credited directly to equity, in which case the deferred tax is also dealt with in equity. Deferred tax assets and liabilities are offset when the company has a legally enforceable right to offset current tax assets and liabilities and the deferred tax assets and liabilities relate to taxes levied by the same tax authority.

Included in 'loans from related parties' per Note 19 is an amount owing to the lender of £5,610,024. Following a Deed of Variation signed in November 2023, a settlement was reached for the company to pay an amount of £5,864,490 to clear this loan over the next 4 years. In the event of default, the company would have a potential liability of £7,377,305. This amount has not been recognised in the accounts as the Board are under the view that default will not arise.

 

Also included in 'loans from related parties' per Note 19 is an amount owing to Kelgary Sports & Entertainment Ltd. Following the year end, it was agreed that Kelgary Sports & Entertainment Ltd would waive its entitlement to repayment of the loan. This on the basis that DMA Holdings SA makes payments due to Old Kent Road Financial Ltd as set out in the agreement dated November 2023. DMA Holdings SA has provided security for all amounts owed to Old Kent Road Financial Ltd.

 

 

Other

 

The company has financial commitments under contract for hardware support and maintenance of pitches at the main ground and academy. The financial commitment amounts to £62,028 as at 30 June 2023.

 

The company may receive, under transfer agreements, further amounts for players already sold dependent on whether these players are sold on again for a profit. No reliable estimate can be made of the likelihood of these players being transferred or their potential transfer values.

 

The company has, under transfer agreements, a liability to pay additional sums dependent on players' attainment and any subsequent transfer value. At the year end such fees were estimated at £590,000 (2022: £913,125) to be payable by the company.

 

No provision has been made in these accounts for such liabilities as management do not consider the above estimate to be reliable as player attainments may not be achieved and therefore the payment may never crystallise.

 

The company is also awaiting the outcome of the Sheldon review in to a number of historic cases. The Directors believe that the Club may have a maximum liability in of £50,000 in respect of this review. No provision has been made in these financial statements.
