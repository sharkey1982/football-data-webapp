REPORT OF THE DIRECTORS
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2021


The directors present their report with the financial statements of the company for the year ended 30 June 2021.

REVIEW OF BUSINESS
Going into this financial year the club and indeed the world was facing one of the most uncertain times from recent memory with the Covid-19 pandemic taking hold. In addition, we had just said thank you and farewell to the Watson Family who had provided significant funding for many years but had decided their time with Rovers must come to an end.

The Club worked on a number of scenarios with the primary focus being to survive the pandemic whilst still allowing the playing squad to be competitive. The Club had already taken the decision to drastically cut costs with 19 positions being made redundant around the start of the financial year in order to save around £350k over the year and opted to accept all support that was available except for those that came with detrimental consequences to the ability to sign players. That support included furlough (although we continued to pay permanent staff at 100%), EFL basic loan, business rates relief, VAT and PAYE deferral and player and staff wage deferral. We also tried to maximise the benefit of all opportunities that arose during the year such as additional Premier League Solidarity payments, iFollow income, additional rental income at the Stadium as well as VAT benefits and Research and Development tax rebates that became available.

It was initially anticipated that the first half of the 20/21 season would be behind closed doors but it later transpired that all matches would be without supporters in attendance.

Although the playing budget was reduced at the start of the season, it was done so with the aim to remain competitive in the league with the knowledge that our League One rivals had gone through a similar exercise. The season started well under Darren Moore with the team sat comfortably in the play-offs for large parts of the first half of the season.

After resisting for at least 3 transfer windows, the Club received what we deemed an acceptable bid for our then captain, Ben Whiteman, meaning we could grant him his wish for a move to a club in a higher division. Within the financial reports the full net proceeds of the transfer are included after accounting for the sell-on payment due to Sheffield United and the value of his initial transfer which hadn't been amortised at the point of the leaving. The payment terms of the deal were structured in such a way that gave us an upfront payment to help get through the pandemic which gave us some certainty at a very difficult time and future instalments to support the playing budget going forward

January also saw a number of incoming players at the manager's request with funds being made available for a promotion push with highly regarded midfielder John Bostock and Striker Omar Bogle making permanent moves to Rovers, supported by 3 new loans and the much anticipated return of Josh Sims.

A short while later the Club received an approach from Sheffield Wednesday for the services of first team manager Darren Moore and his assistant Jamie Smith. The manager took up the opportunity to move to our South Yorkshire neighbours and we were duly compensated in accordance with the terms of the respective employment contracts. The Club have now significantly increased the levels of compensation due within the Football Manager and Assistant Manager contracts to protect the club further without increasing the compensation due should the club seek to terminate these football contracts.

The Club took immediate action to appoint Andy Butler as interim first team manager until the end of the season to give us a level of continuity as the season had gone well to that point.

Sadly, after a bright start under Andy's guidance with 2 wins from his first 2 games, results didn't continue in the same vein and a poor run saw the team drop to a finishing position of 14th in League One.

At the end of the season the Club decided a change of management was required and following an extensive recruitment process, former fans favourite Richie Wellens was appointed to his 4th EFL management position.

Through the proactive approach that was put in place to deal with the pandemic the Club managed to achieve its long-term ambition of sustainability, even through the adversity of the pandemic, with an operating profit of £98k excluding transfer fees. This did come at a cost as there were significant debts accumulated through the period which would need repayment in the short-term.

On behalf of the owners I want to take this opportunity to thank you for your efforts to help us through these unprecedented times.

1. STATUTORY INFORMATION

Doncaster Rovers Limited is a private company, limited by shares , registered in England and Wales. The company's registered number and registered office address can be found on the Company Information page.

2. ACCOUNTING POLICIES

Basis of preparing the financial statements
These financial statements have been prepared in accordance with Financial Reporting Standard 102 "The Financial Reporting Standard applicable in the UK and Republic of Ireland" including the provisions of Section 1A "Small Entities" and the Companies Act 2006. The financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost convention.

Going Concern
The accounts have been prepared on the going concern basis.

The directors consider the basis of the preparation to be appropriate based on their continuing financial support of the company's principal creditors and ultimate controlling parties and its future operating prospects.

The financial statements are prepared in sterling, which is the functional currency of the entity.

Turnover
Turnover represents various income streams associated with operating a professional football club including gate receipts, transfer fees receivable and commercial income.

Transfer and signing on fees
The cost of players' registrations are capitalised and amortised over the period of the respective players' contracts.

Signing on fees are capitalised and amortised in full in the year that they are paid.

Tangible fixed assets
Depreciation is provided at the following annual rates in order to write off each asset over its estimated useful life or, if held under a finance lease, over the lease term, whichever is the shorter.
Land and buildings - Straight line over the life of the lease
Plant and machinery etc - 40% on reducing balance, 25% on reducing balance and 10% - 25% on cost

Government grants
Coronavirus Job Retention Scheme grant income is recognised in the period to which the underlying furloughed staff costs relate to. The payroll liability has been incurred by the entity, and it has therefore met the conditions to claim for that payroll accounting period.

Hire purchase and leasing commitments
Assets obtained under hire purchase contracts or finance leases are capitalised in the balance sheet. Those held under hire purchase contracts are depreciated over their estimated useful lives. Those held under finance leases are depreciated over their estimated useful lives or the lease term, whichever is the shorter.

The interest element of these obligations is charged to profit or loss over the relevant period. The capital element of the future payments is treated as a liability.

Rentals paid under operating leases are charged to profit or loss on a straight line basis over the period of the lease.
